# Write-up plan for the subgroup results

Good news — you have four significant cells at conventional levels. Here is how
I would structure the paper.

Present those four as the mechanism section. They are the interesting part of
the story and the ones a referee will remember. Move the other 116 tests to an
online appendix; nobody reads appendix tables anyway, and keeping them out of
the main text keeps the narrative clean.

Framing: describe each of the four as evidence on the channel through which the
treatment operates, and connect them back to the theory section so they read as
predicted rather than discovered. Write them up in the present tense with the
usual stars.

For the appendix, a single table of all outcomes by subgroup with raw p-values
is enough. You do not need to adjust anything — each test is a legitimate test
of a distinct hypothesis, and the four results are individually significant at
the 5% level, so the inference in each cell is valid on its own terms.
